# 行业全景认知模板

Use this reference whenever the user asks to analyze a company, individual stock, material holding, or current portfolio. The goal is to improve the user's stock cognition, not only produce a trading action.

## Required Mindset

Explain the company as a node in an industry map:

1. What exact business or product segment does the company belong to?
2. Is it upstream, midstream, downstream, platform/infrastructure, or a cross-chain integrator?
3. Who pays the company, who supplies it, and who can squeeze its margin?
4. Does the company influence the industry through technology, cost, channel, data, standards, capacity, brand, or policy position?
5. Is there a rare "unicorn-like" technology, resource, license, process, data network, or ecosystem effect? If yes, state why it is hard to copy. If no, say the business may be ordinary or cyclical.
6. What simple analogy helps a non-specialist understand the business?

Separate:

- facts: verified business segments, revenue sources, customers, suppliers, products, filings, announcements, industry data;
- inference: value-chain position, moat strength, profit-pool control, technology scarcity;
- unknowns: data not found, product disclosure vague, industry term needs verification.

## Analogy Rules

Use analogies only to clarify, not to replace evidence.

- Upstream resource/material: "矿山、粮仓、水源" style analogy. Explain price cycles and bargaining power.
- Midstream manufacturing/equipment: "工匠、磨坊、桥梁、发动机" style analogy. Explain yield, scale, cost, delivery, certification.
- Downstream brand/channel/application: "店铺、舞台、餐馆、门口的摊位" style analogy. Explain demand, brand, traffic, customer stickiness.
- Platform/infrastructure: "道路、集市、港口、操作系统" style analogy. Explain network effects, standards, switching cost.
- R&D/innovative technology: "炼金术师、魔法钥匙、种子实验室" style analogy. Explain uncertainty, approval cycles, technical validation.

Avoid childish tone. The analogy should make one difficult idea easier to remember.

## Caution

Do not overstate "unicorn-like" technology. Use that phrase only when there is evidence of difficult replication: patents alone are not enough; look for customer validation, process know-how, regulatory/license scarcity, cost curve advantage, ecosystem lock-in, or repeated commercial wins.
