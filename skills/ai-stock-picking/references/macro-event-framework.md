# Macro Event Framework

Use this reference when a macro event may change risk appetite, liquidity, valuation multiples, or sector rotation.

## Primary-Source Stack

For central-bank events:

- official decision statement
- projection table or data appendix
- press conference transcript
- prior meeting materials for comparison
- market prices after the event

For data releases:

- official release table
- revisions to prior periods
- consensus expectation and whisper expectation if available
- market reaction after release

For policy events:

- official policy document
- implementation timeline
- affected industries and exemptions
- market expectations before publication

## Expectation Gap

Do not summarize only what happened. Compare:

- what the market expected
- what the event delivered
- how prices reacted
- which sectors have first-order exposure
- which supply-chain links have second-order exposure

## Market Transmission

Track:

- rates and yield curve
- currency and commodities
- equity index breadth
- high-beta and defensive sector behavior
- capital flow indicators relevant to the market
- risk assets such as crypto only as secondary liquidity indicators

## Interpretation Rules

- A headline is not a signal until verified against the official text.
- A speech is not a trade unless price reaction confirms surprise.
- A hawkish statement with risk-on price action means positioning or expectations mattered more than words.
- A dovish statement with risk-off price action may indicate growth fear rather than policy relief.
- Trend across three to six months matters more than one noisy data point.

## Output Classifications

- Risk-on: increase research intensity on growth and cyclicals.
- Risk-off: reduce sizing, prioritize balance-sheet quality and cash flow.
- Rotation: map beneficiaries and losers by policy transmission.
- Wait: event is material but evidence conflicts or market has not digested it.
